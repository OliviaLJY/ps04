# Agent Operations Brief — Editorial Policy (revision 2)

## Audience and relevance

Serve engineering leads at small software teams deciding whether and how to trial AI coding agents. Prefer evidence that changes a workflow decision: task selection, review design, access controls, measurement, cost, or rollback. Exclude general AI news with no plausible engineering-management consequence.

## Freshness and identity

Use only material whose original publication date lies within the run's inclusive 30-day UTC window. Undated, future-dated, and republished old material is mechanically excluded. A podcast and its YouTube upload are one underlying item when they contain the same recording. Syndication and repeated coverage of one announcement do not create independent corroboration.

## Claim-level credibility

Assess evidence for each claim, not the prestige of a publisher or the model's confidence. Identify what was directly observed, the comparison or baseline, sample and setting, method, incentives, and important omissions. Product demonstrations establish that a staged path can work; they do not establish reliability, productivity, security, or return on investment in ordinary teams. Benchmarks are relevant only when their task distribution and review criteria fit the audience's work.

## Novelty and perspective

Prefer a mix of primary product facts, independent technical analysis, and practitioner experience. Cap routine reliance on any one publisher. Preserve relevant negative or null evidence. State missing perspectives, especially maintainers reviewing agent-generated changes, security teams, junior developers, and teams reporting failed deployments.

## Uncertainty, conflict, and incentives

Label reported facts, source claims, and our inferences. When sources conflict, first test whether they use different tasks, baselines, time horizons, or definitions. Do not average incompatible results. Vendor sources may establish product behavior but must be labeled as commercially interested. Unsupported claims are rejected; useful but presently unverifiable claims are deferred. Do not manufacture balance when the evidence points consistently in one direction.

## Revision note

Revision 2 adds an explicit publisher-concentration cap and requires operational measures—review time, failure modes, and total elapsed time—before translating capability claims into adoption advice. The change responds to the first-pass tendency to over-select launch material. Preserve the revision-1 policy in the run-comparison evidence when conducting the required review.
